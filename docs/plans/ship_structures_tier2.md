# Ship Structures Tier 2: Modules, Crew Environment, Condition

## Context
Tier 1 (2026-10-11) breaks every class into parts, but the simulation still runs on class totals. Tier 2
makes the parts matter:
- ships carry **modules** fitted at their home yard;
- the **space environment** acts on crews and tanks: radiation, boil-off, wear;
- **damage** is felt through physics.

The user wants this realistic, compatible with today's economy (market redesign, PR #14: unmet demand 0.6%,
prices 1.50× the reference) or deviating only for good reasons, and measured, with a retune if needed.

User decisions (2026-10-21):
- radiation dose limits each leg and adds hazard pay;
- passengers get their own plan after tier 2 (the cabin slot is reserved);
- liquid hydrogen boils off, and a cryocooler module stops it;
- repairs use the yard's machinery and electronics stock.

The base design is `docs/plans/ship_internals.md` (tier 2 section). This plan replaces its 2b–2e.

## Compatibility principle
- Every new mechanism is a switch in a new `data/economy/ship_environment.csv`, like `market.csv`.
- With the switches off and no modules fitted, a 365-day `--econ-audit` must be **byte-identical** to the
  baseline. Steps A–C must keep that.
- Effects act on quantities the economy already prices: crew wages, provisions per crew-day,
  `decay_fraction_per_day`, α/κ, dry mass, propellant, endurance (`max_mission_days`). So dispatch, contracts,
  orders, investment and refits see them through existing scores, with no new heuristics.
- Deviations, each with its reason:
  1. Radiation: physical crew limits, which the old flat 730 days ignored.
  2. Boil-off: nuclear-thermal ships currently store hydrogen for free.
  3. Maintenance: wear is a real running cost, and the spares create new demand.

  Each comes with a before/after benchmark.

## A. Designs derived in C++ (no gameplay change)
- `Simulation::ship_design(ship)` (`src/simulation/Simulation.cpp:325`, 16 call sites) returns a derived
  `ShipClassDefinition`:
  - the ship's `class_id` (the tank variant stays the base, so part C refits need no rewrite: simpler than the
    old "tanks as a slot" idea);
  - plus `ShipState::fit`, a sorted list of module ids;
  - plus later the condition buckets.
- Derived designs are cached in a `std::map<std::string, ShipClassDefinition> designs_`. The key is the design
  id `class_id[+module…][#condition]`, and map entries keep stable references. The cache is pure: rebuilt from
  data and save, never saved (save-drift lesson, `reference_sanitizers`).
- Derivation in a new `src/domain/ShipDesign.{hpp,cpp}` uses the builder's formulas:
  - dry mass and value are sums of the parts;
  - nuclear-thermal: Δv = Isp·g₀·ln(wet/dry), acceleration = thrust/wet;
  - plasma: α = jet power/dry; crew = berths; hold = the hold parts.
- **Port test:** derive every `_tankNNN` variant from its nominal hull with a tank swap, and match
  `ship_classes.csv` within rounding. Added to `tests/test_main.cpp`.
- Replace the remaining `class_id` keys with the design id:
  - the leg-estimate key (`Simulation.cpp:1709`);
  - probe ships (1719, 3018, 4003).
- **Tier 1 detail, still decompose-don't-change** (needed by damage and the schematic):
  - split the plasma engine into reactor, shadow shield, power conversion, thruster and superconducting
    magnetic nozzle;
  - split the nuclear-thermal engine into core, shadow shield, nozzle and turbopumps;
  - give radiators a heat-pipe segment count.
  - The splits are sourced in `docs/ship_technology.md`. The builder sums stay exact, and
    `tests/test_build_ship_classes.py` still passes.

## C. Refits and investment choose fits
- Generalise `consider_refit` (`Simulation.cpp:2976`). Options are:
  - the neighbouring tank sizes (as now);
  - adding, removing or swapping one module in a free slot.
- Same rule for every option: `(score − current) × REFIT_PAYBACK_DAYS − bill − current × refit_days > 0`, where
  the bill is `refit_cost_fraction × |Δvalue|` plus installation.
- Cap the option count, and order options by a cheap static bound to keep run time. Measure run time.
- Fleet investment (`commission_step`, `Simulation.cpp:4374`) already picks the best tank variant. It also tries
  the top fits of the winning hull.
- Audit report: a fit census, plus refits by module and their gain.
- With every effect off, no module scores better, so the economy must stay identical.

## D. Crew environment: radiation (deviation, user-approved)
Dose model, `src/simulation/CrewEnvironment.{hpp,cpp}`, a pure function of ship state, position and time:
- **GCR:**
  - about 1.8 mSv/day in deep space at solar minimum (Curiosity's RAD cruise measurement, 1.84 mSv/day);
  - modulated by an 11-year solar cycle (roughly halved at maximum);
  - weakly increasing outward;
  - reduced by the habitat's areal density (derived from hull and shield mass over the habitat surface).
  - Shielding helps GCR only modestly: about 20–30% for 20 g/cm² of water (to source).
- **Solar particle events:** a new system-wide event kind in `events.csv`, so stations get a storm too.
  - The dose scales about 1/r² from the Sun.
  - The dose behind thin hull vs a 20 g/cm² shelter follows the August 1972 event spectrum (to source).
  - It draws from a new RNG stream (`event_rng_` seed + 1), so today's station events are unchanged.
- **Trapped radiation at stations:** a per-station `local_dose_msv_per_day` column. LEO is ISS-like (about
  0.3–0.5). Ganymede sits in Jupiter's magnetosphere, at tens of mSv/day on the surface (to source); stations
  are assumed shielded, but a docked ship's crew accrues the dock rate.
- **Effects:**
  - **Leg limit:** a crew is hired at every departure. A leg's projected dose must stay under the tour
    limit (data; default from the ESA career limit, 1 Sv). This joins `max_mission_days` in
    `choose_mission_pass` (:1875). **`find_contract` gets the same check:** today it checks no endurance at all
    (:3531).
  - **Hazard pay:** wage + dose × cr/mSv. The physical value comes from a dose-limited career: career wage
    ÷ career limit, which can be about 20× today's wage in deep space. It is data, measured, and its final
    value is a decision for the user, logged.
  - The planner's time cost (`estimate_leg`, `find_contract`, investment) uses the same projected rate. Merge
    the duplicated time-cost formula (:483, 1388, 1690, 3540, 4125, 4253) into one function first, as a pure
    refactor.
- **Modules:**
  - storm shelter: a water or polyethylene wall around the sleeping quarters, about 20 g/cm², a few tonnes;
  - extra GCR shielding;
  - "water-wall tanks" for plasma ships: the propellant shields the habitat, so the protection falls as the
    tank empties.
- **Measure in two runs:** dose shown only (no effect), then limits plus hazard pay. Also count how many legs
  the limit blocks.

## E. Habitability, hydroponics, cryo bay
- **Habitability premium:** wage × f(habitable volume per crew, gravity), with NASA's ~25 m³/person guidance
  for long missions (to source). Modules:
  - common room (volume);
  - spin habitat on a tether, ≤ 2–4 rpm with the radius derived from the rpm, mass and cost from the
    rpm-radius relation.
- **Hydroponics:** a partial food make-up, lowering `life_support.food` per crew-day. Growing area, lighting
  power (LED, about 2.5–3 µmol/J) and waste heat are derived.
  - Plasma ships draw from the reactor, a small share of the jet power.
  - Nuclear-thermal ships need solar arrays that weaken as 1/r².
  - It honestly pays only far from farms.
- **Cryo / pressurized bay:** lower decay for food and medicine in that bay. This needs per-bay holds in the
  manifest builder (decay applied per bay), so keep it limited to one bay per hull.
- Benchmark after each.

## F. Hydrogen boil-off and the cryocooler (deviation, user-approved)
- **Passive boil-off:** heat leak = MLI flux × tank area. The tank area comes from the volume already in
  `ship_components.csv`, and the flux scales with sunlight 1/r². Boil-off = leak ÷ LH2 latent heat
  (446 kJ/kg). The order is ~0.5–1%/month for a 90 t tank; the flux needs a source (NASA CFM/ZBO studies).
- The loss is applied to `propellant_kg` while in flight and docked. Planners must load extra propellant for it:
  - the Kepler planner gets a boil-off term in its propellant budget;
  - the 10% reserve stays.
- **Cryocooler module (zero boil-off):** a 20 K cryocooler at about 1% of Carnot (W input per W lifted, to
  source), plus solar array mass and power.
- Plasma water tanks: no boil-off. Depot fuel already decays at 0.0002/day; leave that.

## G. Advanced radiators (Set B)
Drive module: 10 kW/kg radiators at 747 K, already derived in `ship_technology.md`. It raises α and κ, so
plasma trajectories come out faster. Expensive. Benchmark it.

## H. Condition, wear, damage, repairs
- `ShipState::condition`: part id → 0..1.
- **Wear:** the reactor or engine by operating time; ECLSS by crew-days.
- **Micrometeoroid punctures:** radiator punctures at a flux-model rate (Grün flux per m² per year, scaled
  with r, to source). A puncture loses one heat-pipe segment.
- **Ship events** (`ship_damage` kind, own RNG stream): radiator hit, reactor scram, ECLSS fault.
- **Felt through physics:**
  - Plasma jet power ≤ remaining heat rejection × (1 − φ)/φ, which lowers α, κ and speed.
  - A nuclear-thermal engine's wear lowers thrust.
  - An ECLSS fault raises the provisions used per crew-day.
  - The design id carries condition in 10% buckets, so caches stay bounded.
- **Repairs at the home yard:**
  - the bill = price × damage × factor;
  - repairs **consume the yard's machinery and electronics units** at the station price. A lack of stock
    delays the repair, and the shortfall becomes a station purchase order, so the existing order system
    hauls the spares;
  - `consider_refit` weighs repair against carrying on.
- **Modules:** extra radiator segments (redundancy), and a workshop with a spares kit (in-flight repair of
  part of the damage, costs mass).
- Comms log lines for damage and repairs.
